PROBLEM STATEMENT & COMMUTER REALITY

Why Conventional GPS Fails Deadline-Bound Commuters

The Average-Time Illusion

  • Google Maps & Apple Maps optimize only for static average or shortest distance.
  • They assume constant road conditions, ignoring transit delay variance and traffic volatility.
  • A 10-minute bottleneck cascades into missed connections and ruined schedules.

The Cost of Being Late

  • Students face strict 75% attendance cutoffs and locked exam hall doors.
  • Workers face salary dock-ins, missed client pitches, and disciplinary penalties.
  • Commuters suffer severe stress watching unpredictable bus and train schedules.

Zero Proactive Recovery

  • Current navigation tools never provide a "Latest Safe Departure" (LSD) countdown.
  • When transit is missed, existing apps offer no automated contingency plan.
  • Users need on-time arrival certainty, not just nominal travel minutes.
💡 THE REALITY: Conventional GPS answers "Which route is shortest?" — Commuters urgently need: "Will I arrive before my hard deadline?"

ALGORITHMIC INNOVATION

A* Road Routing & Penalty-Based Diverse Alternatives

ADMISSIBLE A* ROAD ROUTING

  • Haversine Distance Heuristic:
    h(u, v) = Haversine(u, v) / Max_Speed (80 km/h)
    Dividing spherical distance by maximum permissible velocity guarantees the heuristic is strictly admissible (never overestimates travel time).
  • Spatial Snap Lookup:
    Pre-loads regional road graphs into memory. Uses fast spatial k-d tree lookup to snap GPS coordinates to the nearest network node.

PENALTY METHOD & OVERLAP FILTER

  • Iterative Edge Cost Inflation:
    Cost'(e) = Cost(e) * (1 + Penalty_Factor)
    Traversed edges are penalized by 30-50%, forcing A* into distinct highway and bypass corridors on successive iterations.
  • Strict Overlap Rejection (< 80%):
    Calculates Jaccard path edge overlap against previously accepted routes. Any candidate sharing >80% path length is strictly rejected.
MATHEMATICAL MODELING

Stochastic Monte Carlo Simulation & Reliability Scoring

Monte Carlo Engine (1,000 Runs)

  • Every road segment has an expected duration (μ) and variance (σ).
  • Simulates 1,000 independent random commuter journeys per route.
  • Captures surprise bottlenecks, railway crossings, and peak rush delays.

On-Time Probability & P90

  • OTP % = (Runs arriving before deadline / 1,000) * 100%.
  • P90 Arrival: Arrival time guaranteed in 90% of simulated journeys.
  • LSD: Latest Safe Departure = Deadline - P90 Travel Duration.

Reliability Scoring Formula

  • Score = Buffer - (w1 * Variability) - (w2 * Wait Time) - (w3 * Transfers)
  • Configurable weights prioritize arrival buffer and penalize schedule instability.
  • Provides a single 0-100 reliability index for instant decision-making.
🎯 MATHEMATICAL ASSURANCE: Commuters never depend on "best-case" estimates — they get statistical proof of on-time arrival before stepping out of the door.
MACHINE LEARNING & SELF-CALIBRATION

The Self-Calibrating Commute: Bayesian Feedback Loop

STEP 01

Empirical Telemetry

Commuters log actual departure & arrival times via the /log endpoint. Trip logs record predicted duration vs. actual road transit time, capturing seasonal bottlenecks.

STEP 02

Welford's Algorithm

Updates running segment mean (μ) and variance (σ²) in a single pass without re-training heavy neural networks: M_k = M_{k-1} + (x_k - M_{k-1})/k. Prevents numerical instability.

STEP 03

Adaptive Intelligence

As more commuters log trips, simulation accuracy increases automatically. Regional road works, monsoon delays, and checkpoint slowdowns reflect dynamically in future OTP % scores.

🔒 PRIVACY BY DESIGN: All trip calibration is aggregated locally into SQLite segment metrics — zero personal movement tracking or third-party advertising profiling.
